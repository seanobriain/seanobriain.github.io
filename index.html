Recent Lateral Research Breakthroughs

Documented examples of how local geography, naming conventions, and collateral records resolve genealogical dead ends.

Co. Clare • 1810s Surname Shift

Unmasking a Gaelic-to-English Surname Translation

A client’s ancestor vanished prior to 1840 under the surname “Green.” By analysing townland neighbours and traditional naming patterns in East Clare, I proved the family transitioned from Ó hUaithnín (Honeen) to Green across two generations.

✓ Linked 4 earlier generations via Tithe & Estate rolls
West Cork • Pre-Famine Homestead Pinpoint

Pinpointing an Exact Townland from “County Cork”

US naturalisation papers listed only “Co. Cork.” By clustering the rare combination of the mother’s maiden surname and baptismal sponsors in Boston, I isolated a single coastal townland in Carbery West and located the family cabin on the 1842 OS map.

✓ Exact ancestral holding mapped for family visit
Co. Tyrone • 1740s 18th-Century Deeds

Bridging the 1700s Gap via Registry of Deeds Memorials

Where local Presbyterian session books had not survived, unindexed memorials of three-life leases in the Dublin Registry of Deeds named three generations of fathers and sons simultaneously—extending the pedigree back to 1718.

✓ Full transcription & lease lineage report delivered
