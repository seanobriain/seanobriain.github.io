Scholar’s Note • Dublin MS. REF. 1650–1922

Why Standard Database Searches Stall in Ireland

The 1922 Public Record Office fire at Dublin’s Four Courts destroyed most 19th-century census returns and pre-1858 wills—leading many researchers to assume their Irish trail ends abruptly.

Yet parallel manuscripts—Registry of Deeds memorials, estate rental rolls, Tithe Applotment books, and parish registers—survive in abundance when approached through lateral evidence and local context.

CASE I • LEINSTER (1742) REGISTRY OF DEEDS

Overcoming the 1922 Public Record Office Fire via Deeds Memorials

With local 18th-century wills and census returns lost in the 1922 Four Courts fire, an unindexed memorial of a three-life lease in Dublin’s Registry of Deeds explicitly recited three generations of fathers, sons, and married daughters—restoring a broken 1700s pedigree.

✓ THREE GENERATIONS VERIFIED IN ONE DEED
CASE II • MAYO & GALWAY GRIFFITH’S VALUATION

Untangling Common Surnames via Naming Patterns & Land Rolls

In a Connacht parish where twelve distinct households shared the same surname, applying traditional Irish patronymic naming patterns alongside Griffith’s Valuation holding clusters and baptismal sponsors isolated the exact ancestral cabin and sub-tenancy.

✓ EXACT TOWNLAND HOLDING ISOLATED
CASE III • MUNSTER (c. 1665) HEARTH MONEY ROLLS

Tracing a Munster Family Back to 1660s Hearth Money Rolls

By working backward sequentially through 19th-century estate rentals, 18th-century Chancery equity bills, and 1660s Hearth Money Rolls, a Munster lineage was anchored to its mid-17th-century barony and ancestral parish origin.
